=== tfsa or rrsp for a student?
Both are registered accounts that help investments grow with tax advantages, but they work in opposite ways.

**TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account)**
- You contribute money you've already paid tax on, so there's no deduction.
- Growth and withdrawals are **tax-free**.
- You can withdraw any time, and the amount you take out is added back to your room on January 1 of the next year.

**RRSP (Registered Retirement Savings Plan)**
- Contributions are **tax-deductible**, which lowers your taxable income now.
- Growth is tax-sheltered, but withdrawals are taxed as **income**.
- It's meant for retirement, so withdrawals generally start after age 60.

**Which one fits you?**
- **Lower income now, expect higher income later:** the deduction in an RRSP can help now, and withdrawals later are taxed at your then-higher income.
- **Higher income now, no clear plan for later:** the TFSA offers flexibility and simplicity.
- **Saving for something in the next few years:** either one, since withdrawals don't count against your annual room.

**Contribution limits**
- Both have annual and lifetime maximums that change over time, and your personal limit is shown in your CRA My Account. Many people use a combination of the two.

Les taux d'intérêt, c'est le prix que tu paies pour emprunter de l'argent, ou gagnes pour prêter de l'argent.

Quand tu empruntes :
- Le taux d'intérêt fixe, c'est le taux qui reste le même pendant le terme, donc tes paiements sont prévisibles.

- Le taux d'intérêt fixe, c'est le montant que tu gagnes, whatever happens.
- Le taux d'intérêt variable peut te donner plus ou moins, selon le taux du prêteur.

Le plus sûr, c'est souvent le fixe; le plus risqué, le variable. Ton horizon et ton confort avec le risque sont importants.
